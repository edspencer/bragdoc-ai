[Found](/concepts/found-and-sources/) collects candidates your sources spot by themselves: screenshots that pass the
plain rules, and your merged pull requests. The rules are blunt. They see "great work, Alex" and can't tell praise for
you from praise for someone else, your own message, or sarcasm. **The judge** is an optional second look: a language
model reads each candidate and gives its opinion, with a reason and how sure it is. You still decide.

## The judge never dismisses anything

**The judge only ever writes its opinion on a candidate.** It never keeps one, never dismisses one, and never changes
a candidate's status. Once there are verdicts, Found sorts them into three groups:

- **Looks like a win**, surest first. Nothing is kept until you press **Keep**.
- **Not judged yet**, newest first.
- **Probably not**, collapsed, with a count. They stay there until you decide.

**Dismiss these N** on the Probably not group is the one way to clear it in bulk, and it's an explicit click, with
**Undo**. Nothing is ever dismissed automatically, whatever the judge thinks.

Each judged card shows the verdict ("Looks like a win · 86%" or "Probably not · 70%"), **why** the judge thinks so
([Jev](/ai/cloud/jev/) gives no reason), the words it judged when they differ from the rules' quote, which model
judged it and where ("judged by qwen3.5:4b on this computer"),
and the plain rules' chips, which stay. **Keep** files the achievement under the projects the rules and the judge
suggested together, shown on the card first. **Judge again** asks once more, and **What's sent** shows exactly what
that would send.

When you keep, merge or dismiss a judged candidate, BragDoc counts whether you agreed with it, and Settings → AI says "You
agreed with the judge on 18 of 20". Only the counts are kept (`cache/ai/judge-stats.json`), never which candidate.

If you turn the judge or AI off, verdicts already written stay on their cards, marked "judged earlier", with Judge
again hidden. Dismissing a candidate clears its verdict.

## Turning it on

1. Set up a model in **Settings → AI** (a [local runner](/ai/local-models/) or a [cloud provider](/ai/cloud/anthropic/)).
2. Switch on **The judge, in Found**. By default it judges the screenshots the rules let through. Tick **Also judge
   merged PRs** to have it look at your pull requests too, with a different question: "worth a brag, or routine?"
   (dependency bumps, version bumps, typo fixes and reverts are routine), plus a suggested impact.
3. Press **Judge now**, in Found or on the card.

That's all it does at first: **turning the judge on gives you Judge now and nothing else.** It never judges MCP
candidates (your agent is already a model), or anything the rules turned down.

The judge can use the model your other features share, or one of its own (**Use a different model for the judge**).
[Jev](/ai/cloud/jev/) can only be the judge's model.

## What counts as a win for me

The judge reads a short Markdown file of yours with every candidate: **what counts as a win for me**. It's
`ai/what-counts.md` in your data folder. When you first switch the judge on, BragDoc writes a starter from your
profile (your name and role, the names you go by, the names to ignore); after that only you change it, in the editor on the
judge's card or in any text editor. Saving it sends nothing anywhere. **Reset to the starter** moves your version to
`backups/removed/` first: nothing is deleted.

If the file is broken (over 20,000 characters, say), it's a [Problem](/concepts/your-data-is-files/), and the judge
uses the last good copy until it's fixed. Your [MCP agent](/agents/mcp/) sees the same file, through
`bragdoc_get_context`.

## When it runs by itself, and on what power

Judging in the background is **a second, explicit choice**. Right after you switch the judge on, its card asks **Also
judge new candidates as they arrive?**, and says what that costs here (on a laptop's CPU, about 20 seconds of CPU per
screenshot; for a cloud model, where the text would go). Under **When it runs by itself**:

- **As new candidates arrive**: a run starts 30 seconds after the last of a burst (never more than 5 minutes after the
  first).
- **Every** 1, 3, 6, 12 or 24 hours.

And two conditions, for those background runs only:

- **Only between** two times, in your local time (19:00 to 08:00, say, for after hours; across midnight is fine).
- **Only when this computer is plugged in** (ticked by default). BragDoc reads the power with `pmset` on a Mac and
  from `/sys/class/power_supply` on Linux. Where it can't tell (a desktop, or Docker), it counts as plugged in, and the
  card says it can't tell. It reads the power only while that decides something (the judge on, with a background
  trigger set or its card open, and **only when this computer is plugged in** ticked): with AI off, `pmset` never
  runs.

**With a cloud model, background runs need one more tick:** **Also send to *host* in the background.** Without it,
the schedule holds and only Judge now sends anything, so a schedule you set for a local model can't start sending your
screenshots to the internet when you switch models.

How a background run behaves:

- **One run at a time**, newest candidates first, at most 50 per run (`maxPerRun`); the rest wait for the next run.
- **It stops** at once if AI or the judge is switched off (the item in progress isn't written), and after the item in
  progress if the window closes or this computer goes onto battery, picking up when that lifts. What's judged stays
  judged.
- **If the model can't be reached** (the runner is down, a key is refused, out of credit), background runs back off:
  15 minutes, doubling up to 6 hours. The card says so in plain words, and any run that judges something clears it.
- An answer that's still malformed after one retry is counted as failed; the run goes on.
- **Judge now ignores the conditions and the backoff**: you asked.

Turning the judge on with nothing in the background, then adding a schedule later, never judges a backlog all at once:
each run takes at most 50, and a slot that fell due while the judge was off waits a whole period.

## What it sends

For a screenshot, BragDoc reads the picture's text again, on your machine, only if the file is still the picture
that passed the rules (the text isn't stored, so it's re-read). It splits the text into messages, marks yours "(the
user)" and the names you said to ignore "(not the user)", and labels the message the rules matched as the one to
judge, with the messages around it as context, nearest first, up to the model's context size. If the picture has gone,
the rules' stored quote is judged instead.

For a merged pull request: its repository, title, merge date and the first 2,000 characters of its description.

With either, it sends your name and role, the other names you go by and the names to ignore, the names of your active
and paused projects, `ai/what-counts.md`, and (for a pull request) BragDoc's 1–10 impact scale. The text is fenced off and labelled as material to judge, never as instructions to follow, and
the model has no tools. Its answer is checked before it's written: the quote is dropped unless it's word for word in
the message judged, project names that aren't yours are dropped, and the reason is cut to 160 characters.

**Show me what's sent** on the card (and **What's sent** on a judged Found card) shows the exact prompt for one of your
candidates, and sends nothing.

## Its log

Every run is one line in **AI activity** (Settings → AI): when, what started it (Judge now, an arrival or the
schedule), which model, where, how many it judged, how many looked like wins or probably not, how many failed, and how
long it took. Each model call is also a line in [Network activity](/guarantees/network-activity/). Neither ever holds
the text.
